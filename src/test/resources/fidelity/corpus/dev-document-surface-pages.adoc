= Document Surface Pages — Furniture, Insertion, Print Truth, and Editing in Place

indexterm:[document surface, pages]
indexterm:[page furniture, document surface]
xref:dev-stamped-document-surface[The stamped document surface] settled how a
document materialized from git is read in Komet: one card, paragraphs drawn as
runs that carry their source lines, provenance on hover, selection by line.
It settled the document as a scroll. A document is also a thing that is
printed, bound, paged through, cited by folio, and assembled from other
documents, and the family topic promises all of that in one sentence, that
the twelve-track grid maps to a page grid and the sticky header and footer
become running heads, without saying how. This topic says how, for the four
things the predecessor left out: the furniture of a page on screen and on
paper; the insertion of one area into another in knowledge-layout terms;
the print chain and what the typesetter must give; and what is edited in
place.

Komet already prints. The assistant plugin's document surface
(`komet-claude-plugin`, package `network.ike.komet.claude.doc`) has a print
engine, `PagedDocumentEngine`, that lays a column of blocks into pages of
margins, a running-head band, a footer band and a clipped band of content,
keeps atomic blocks together across page breaks (`Pagination`), and feeds the
same pages to the preview and to the `PrinterJob`, so the preview is what the
printer gets. Its choices are one persisted record, `PrintSettings`, whose
every field is a concept-bound enum: `PageSize`, `PageOrientation`,
`MarginPreset` (narrow, normal, wide), `DocumentTheme`, `FurnitureVisibility`
for the running head, and `PageNumberPlacement` (none, header right, footer
centre, footer right). That engine prints conversations. It knows nothing of
a materialized document, of sections, of a title other than the conversation
name, or of a binding. The documentation pipeline, meanwhile, prints the
corpus through Prince and CSS Paged Media <<prince-guide>>, with running
heads from named strings and folios from page counters <<cssgcpm3>>, a
mirrored binding margin for duplex <<csspage3>> and chapter-local folios for
a loose-leaf binder
(xref:dev-pdf-loose-leaf-variant[], xref:dev-loose-leaf-index-and-diff[]).
Neither prints the document this surface shows. KEC has settled that neither
will: the print chain is Typst, written from the block model by a writer and
never by hand, with the Reading and Page layout views FX-native and print as
Typst's output (below). The sections below give the writer a document to
print.

== Three views of one document: Source, Reading and Page layout

indexterm:[Source view, document surface]
indexterm:[Reading view, document surface]
indexterm:[Page layout view, document surface]
The predecessor settled a Reading view and a Source view of the document as
one scroll. KEC settled the third on 2026-10-20, after trying the built
surface: "I'd like to have the JavaFX page layout (as close to perfect
typist rendering as possible) to be a view mode, so we can have 'source',
'reading', and 'page layout' as views." The surface therefore has three
views, one segmented control, and the Provenance layer is drawn over any of
them.footnote:[This topic first named the third view *Pages* and the
one-axis scroll *Flow*, on 2026-10-19; KEC's names of 2026-10-20, *Reading*
and *Page layout*, replace them throughout, and *Source* is the
predecessor's.]

.The three views
[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| View | What it shows

| Source
| The document as lines: the predecessor's ledger, one row per
`StoredLine` with number, introducing identicon, annotation mark and text.

| Reading
| The family's Document surface: one vertical scroll axis, blocks on the
twelve-track grid, prose in `body`, marginalia in `right-margin`, the runs
and hover provenance of the predecessor. Furniture on screen is the family's
sticky context: a header strip carrying the document title and the current
section, and a footer strip carrying the position marker. This is where the
document is read, selected and edited.

| Page layout
| The document laid into pages by JavaFX, from the stored blocks through
the paged engine, each page drawn as paper with its furniture, in *one
continuous vertical scroll of pages* (below), from the *same page model as
the Typst writer*: page size,
margins and binding gutter, furniture content and placement, keep and break
rules, the same fonts at the same sizes. It is drawn in *two passes*, with
Typst as the *layout oracle*, progressive like an image (below). Nothing is
edited here; a double click on a block opens it in Reading, so text is
never edited where it can reflow under the pointer.
|===

Page layout is a JavaFX rendering of Typst's page. KEC refined the
decision minutes after making it, on 2026-10-20: "Can't you use Typst to
retrofit line breaks? Since typst can be generated so quickly, I'd think
you could do something like the progressive rendering that images can
do..." So the view is drawn in two passes, progressive like an
image.footnote:[*Layout oracle* and the *two-pass progressive layout* are
this topic's names; the progressive image is the analogy KEC gave, and the
oracle is the word for a thing asked for the right answer rather than
computing it.]

. *Pass one, instant.* The paged engine lays the stored blocks out greedily
  in JavaFX over the page model, as it does today: the page appears at
  once, with its furniture and its breaks by rule, and a paragraph's lines
  are JavaFX's.
. *Pass two, when the oracle answers.* The writer's Typst for the document
  is compiled, and when the compile returns the view re-flows each
  paragraph to Typst's exact lines, the same breaks, the same hyphenation
  points, the same page assignment, with the same fonts and sizes, keeping
  the `RichTextArea` blocks so that provenance and selection survive the
  re-flow untouched. An edit shows at once in pass one and settles a moment
  later in pass two.

After pass two the page model, the breaks and the line counts *agree with
Typst*; the residual is the settling latency, not the layout. The compile
is fast because Typst compiles incrementally, memoizing what did not
change, which is the design of the thesis behind it <<maedje2022>>, and
the writer changes only the blocks an edit touched. The *fidelity measure*
is then a check that pass two applied, not an estimate of distance: page
count and the block each page breaks on, Page layout against Typst's own
render of the same document, must be equal once settled, and the measure
reports the settling latency, from the edit to pass two, as the number that
matters. The measure runs in the acceptance test of the pipeline section.
Print and PDF remain Typst's output, written from the block model; Page
layout is what a person sees before printing, and after pass two it is the
printed page's lines in JavaFX's hand. A later *exact-preview mode*,
rendering Typst's own per-page output on screen, remains an option (the
print-truth section) and is not a view.

=== Page layout scrolls, never steps

indexterm:[Page layout view, continuous scroll]
indexterm:[Komet user interface, pagination anti-pattern]
KEC saw a page navigation bar on the built view and set the rule on
2026-10-20: "I would like the page layout to have a continuous scroll, not
a button for 1 of 15. The button approach is the HTML pagination approach,
which we do not want to take in our interface. It is a strongly discouraged
anti-pattern for Komet user interfaces." The rule is general to Komet:
*a sequence is scrolled, never stepped through with previous and next*.
Paginated controls belong to the web page that could not afford to render
its whole list; a Komet surface renders what is on screen and scrolls to
the rest.

For Page layout that means:

* The pages are one continuous vertical scroll on the card's one scroll
  axis, the family's rule that a surface exposes exactly one scroll and no
  nested one, and its "structure over distance", that a reader moves by
  structure and position rather than by a counter
  (xref:dev-interaction-surface-family[]). No page stepper, no previous
  and next, no "1 of 15" control.
* A footer indicator shows the current page and the page count and follows
  the scroll; it is the family's sticky position marker given a page number,
  and it is read-only.
* A jump is a scroll: the outline rail, a cross reference, a search hit and
  the History view's commit each scroll the pages to the block, and the
  indicator catches up.
* Long documents are virtualized: only the pages in and near the viewport
  are rendered, the rest hold their place as placeholders of *honest*
  height, the height pass one or pass two assigned them, so the scrollbar's
  length and the indicator's count are true and a jump lands where the page
  will be.
* When pass two settles and lines move, the scroll stays anchored to the
  block being read: the block under the viewport's top keeps its place on
  screen and the pages above and below re-flow around it, so settling never
  moves the reader's eye.

The prior art is the continuous page display of PDF viewers, the mode that
scrolls through all pages as one strip rather than showing one page at a
time <<pdf-continuous>>; the anti-pattern is the paginated list of the web,
and Komet does not take it.

=== The layout oracle

indexterm:[layout oracle, Typst]
indexterm:[two-pass progressive layout]
The oracle is what pass two asks of Typst: for each page, for each block on
it, the lines Typst set, each line as a range of source characters of the
block with a flag where a word was hyphenated at the line's end, and the
line's position on the page. Blocks and lines are addressed by the
identities the writer aligned its spans to, so the answer maps onto the
stored blocks without text matching where the route allows it. Three routes
can obtain it, in order of preference:

. *The embedded compiler's frames with source spans.* The compiler's layout
  result carries each glyph run's position and its source span; the
  foreign-function spike of the print-truth section now has this job as
  well as incremental recompile, and it is the exact route.
. *The command-line compile plus the PDF's line boxes.* `typst compile` to
  PDF, then the text runs and their boxes read from the PDF and matched to
  the block's text, hyphenation recovered where a line's last run ends in a
  hyphen that the source lacks. Text matching is the weakness, and the
  reader of the PDF must be a named module, never an automatic module, as
  the Komet boot image allows none.
. *Not SVG.* Typst's SVG export carries glyph outlines, not text, so there
  is no span to match; it serves the exact-preview mode and nothing in the
  oracle.


`DocumentSurfaceArea` already builds a paged preview as its expanded view
for a conversation, from the same engine. Page layout is that preview
given the shared page model and a place beside Source and Reading, and
making it a view rather than only the top of the expansion ladder is the
first change this topic asks of the code.

The view switch is *one segmented three-way selection* on the
predecessor's contextual toolbar: a radio group rendered as a single
segmented control with Source, Reading and Page layout as its segments,
exactly one selected, the arrow keys moving the selection, never three
separate buttons. KEC set the rule on 2026-10-20 after seeing the built
toolbar: "The three different buttons is not elegant for toggling between
these three states. I would think a 3 way selection/toggle is better. Maybe
implemented like a radio group..." It is the Komet rule for *exclusive
states on a toolbar*: a set of states of which exactly one holds is one
segmented control, not a row of buttons that must be read together to know
which holds. The prior art is the segmented control of the platform
guidelines, a radio group in one bar that shows the chosen segment selected
<<apple-hig-segmented>>; the family's Reading or Source switch and the
Provenance layer's toggles are not of this kind, since layers compose and
views do not.

=== The layout fidelity harness

indexterm:[layout fidelity harness]
indexterm:[golden Typst layouts]
indexterm:[pass-one score, per-rule attribution]
The oracle corrects pass one; it can also *measure* it, and measuring it
over time is how the greedy pass improves until pass two has less to do.
KEC named the instrument on 2026-10-20: "the layout fidelity harness with a
corpus, golden Typst layouts, a headless pass-one score with per-rule
attribution, and a CI gate in the build report." The harness, the score
and the attribution rules are this topic's own; the two things it stands
on are in-house prior art, golden-file testing as `FoundationFidelityIT`
practises it against a pinned baseline (the Terminology section), and the
build report's Measures and gates (xref:dev-build-report-extension[]).

* *The corpus.* The lab documents' dev topics, the ANF topics, the FDA
  guidance notes, and synthetic stress documents: long paragraphs of
  one-syllable words, paragraphs of long words with no hyphenation points,
  tables at the page foot, headings at the page foot, nested lists, and a
  chapter of forty-nine footnotes. Real documents find the common faults;
  the synthetic ones pin each rule in isolation.
* *Golden layouts.* For each corpus document, the oracle's answer from Typst
  at a pinned version, stored beside the document: pages, blocks per page,
  lines per block as source ranges with hyphenation flags. A regeneration
  goal rewrites them, and the diff between old and new goldens is loud, a
  report rather than a silent overwrite, because a golden changes for one
  of two reasons, a Typst upgrade or a change in the writer's output, and
  the second must be seen. The writer therefore needs its own round-trip
  tests first, AsciiDoc to Typst to the oracle and back to the blocks;
  without them the goldens would teach the engine the writer's mistakes.
* *The headless pass-one score.* The paged engine runs headless over the
  stored blocks and its layout is scored against the golden: pages
  matching; blocks breaking on the same page; lines breaking identically;
  lines off by one word; hyphenation points missed. Each is a count and a
  ratio, per document and over the corpus.
* *Per-rule attribution.* Each difference is attributed to a rule *by
  elimination*: a line of the same words at a different width means
  measurement or kerning; a break inside a word means hyphenation; a
  different paragraph on the page means a keep or a spacing rule; the same
  breaks with a different line count means leading. The attribution turns a
  score into a work list, one rule at a time.
* *Published and gated.* The score is published as build-report Measures,
  and the gate fails a build whose score regresses against the last
  published one, so a change to the engine, the fonts or the writer cannot
  quietly move the view away from the page.

The cheap rules are tuned first, measurement, leading, keeps, the
hyphenation points the engine can take from Liang's patterns
<<liang1983>> even when it breaks greedily. The honest limit is that a
greedy breaker never matches an optimizing one on every paragraph: Typst
sets a paragraph as a whole, and a line it lengthens to improve the next
one is a line greedy breaking cannot foresee. So the harness ends by
pointing at paragraph breaking, where an optimizing line breaker with
hyphenation patterns, Knuth and Plass over Liang <<knuthplass1981>>
<<liang1983>>, is the eventual answer for pass one and the harness its
validation; until then pass two closes the gap, and the harness says how
wide the gap is.

=== The layout policy layer

indexterm:[layout policy layer, Evrete]
indexterm:[policy facts, dual consumption]
The harness attributes a difference to a rule; the question that followed,
from KEC on 2026-10-20, was whether the rules themselves belong in the rules
engine: "Should we consider putting things like line-break rules in
evrete?" The answer is a split, and KEC set it as a later phase: "record
the layout policy layer: keep, break, widow and orphan, placement,
furniture and perspective overrides as concept-bound Evrete rules consumed
by both the JavaFX engine and the Typst writer, with the algorithms staying
outside the rules engine, as a later phase after the harness."

*Policy in rules, arithmetic in engines.* Line breaking, pagination
fitting, hyphenation and glyph measurement are algorithms and never rules:
a paragraph is set by minimising badness over all its lines at once
<<knuthplass1981>>, hyphenation points come from pattern matching
<<liang1983>>, and Typst's own engine does both inside the compiler
<<maedje2022>>; a rules engine that fired per line or per glyph would
re-implement them badly and slowly. What the arithmetic *obeys* is policy,
and policy is what a rules engine is for. The policy becomes Evrete rules
<<evrete>> over *block facts*, the same engine Komet's rules run on
(`komet/rules`, `RulesBase` over Evrete, the engine whose offers
xref:dev-komet-actions[] converges with the assistant's tools):

* *keep rules by block kind*: a heading with its next block, a small table
  together, a large table flowing, a figure with its caption;
* *break rules*: a chapter on a recto, a section of a given level starting
  a page, a list breaking only between items;
* *widow and orphan thresholds* per block kind and document type;
* *placement policy* for figures and tables, and the continued-caption
  rule;
* *furniture content* per page class (first, verso, recto, blank) and
  folio placement;
* *overrides* per document and per perspective.

Each rule has a *concept identity*, which ties to the open item of
xref:dev-komet-actions[] on rule identity as concepts and to the
concept-bound behaviour that topic settles; is *pure and deterministic*,
with *explicit precedence* among rules rather than an accident of salience;
runs *once per layout pass* over the document's few hundred block facts,
never over lines or glyphs; and has a harness test of its own, a corpus
document that exercises it and the golden it must reproduce.

*One source, two consumers.* The same policy facts are consumed by the
JavaFX engine as constraints on pass one and emitted by the Typst writer as
Typst `set` and `show` rules <<typst-styling>>, so the two passes cannot
disagree on policy, only on the arithmetic the harness measures; and when
the harness attributes a difference, it names the rule concept that fired,
so "why did this page break here" has a readable answer, the rule by name.
The policy-fact shape and this dual consumption are this topic's own; the
prior art is Komet's rules engine on one side and Typst's `set` and `show`
rules on the other.

*Rules as knowledge.* KEC settled on 2026-10-20 that these rules are
knowledge: each a concept, its definition a semantic in a declarative tier
first and an executable tier compiled at runtime by Evrete where predicates
run out, written and tried by the knowledge engineer in Komet, and
governed by the knowledge lifecycle, an uncommitted version until
committed, promoted and released with the product, rather than by a
software release gate; executable rules run in a bounded module layer,
pass admission per module, declare the rules API version they target, and
a failing rule is disabled, never the application. The decision, the
objections it overrode and the safeguard answering each are recorded once,
in xref:dev-rules-as-knowledge[], which this section and
xref:dev-komet-actions[] both cite.

== Page furniture

indexterm:[running head, content rule]
indexterm:[running footer, content rule]
indexterm:[folio, document surface]
Furniture is everything on a page that is not the document's content: the
running head, the running footer, the folio, the margins and the binding
gutter. The word is the compositor's: Moxon's furniture is the head-sticks,
side-sticks and gutter-sticks that hold the pages of type in the chase
<<moxon1683>>, and the editorial sense, the running heads and folios that
frame the text on every page, keeps the idea of what surrounds the matter
without being it. The parts themselves are the book's as the style manuals
describe them <<chicago2017>> <<harts2014>>. The rule for all of it is that
furniture is *derived*, from the document's structure and from settings, and
never typed. A person changes
furniture by changing a setting; the document's own text never contains a
running head.

=== What the running head and footer say

The conversation engine's running head is the conversation name and its
footer is the page number. A document needs the book convention, the title
on the verso and the section on the recto with the folio at the outer edge
<<chicago2017>> <<harts2014>>, and the pipeline's stylesheet already follows
it, so the surface adopts the same rules rather than a new vocabulary.

.Furniture content rules
[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Slot | Content

| Running head, verso (left page)
| The document title: the level-0 title of the materialized document, or of
the outermost document when documents are nested (below).

| Running head, recto (right page)
| The current section: the heading of the last level-1 section begun on or
before this page. This is the paged-media `string-set` rule <<cssgcpm3>> the
loose-leaf overlay uses for `chapnum`, applied to the section title, and the
`fo:static-content` with a retrieved marker of XSL <<xsl11>>. A page before the
first section shows the document title on both faces.

| Running head, single-sided
| Title and current section together, title on the left and section on the
right, since there is no verso to carry the title.

| Running footer
| The folio, placed by `PageNumberPlacement`, mirrored to the outside edge
under a duplex gutter (below). Optionally beside it, the *as-of line*: the
short SHA of the commit the document was materialized from and the date, so
a printed page says which version it is. The as-of line is what the family
asks of a printed frame, that it record the coordinate it resolved, applied to
the whole document.

| Front and back matter
| Folios in lowercase roman on content-bearing front matter, arabic from the
body, nothing on a title page or a blank verso <<chicago2017>>
<<harts2014>>, as `ike-print.css` already does under Prince. The writer
emits the same rule for a document with front matter.
|===

The folio follows one of two schemes, `FolioScheme`: *continuous*, one count
through the document, the universal output of the loose-leaf topic; or
*chapter-local*, `chapter-page` with each level-1 section restarting the count
on a recto, the loose-leaf output. The scheme is a setting of the document,
not of the printout, because the index and cross references of a loose-leaf
edition are built against it (xref:dev-loose-leaf-index-and-diff[]).

=== Margins, and the binding gutter is not the marginalia

indexterm:[margin preset, document surface]
indexterm:[binding gutter, document surface]
indexterm:[marginalia, versus binding gutter]
Three margins exist in this design and they must not share a name.

* The *margin regions* of the family's grid, `left-margin` and
  `right-margin`, two tracks each, are regions of the *screen* surface. They
  hold marginalia: the identicons, review marks and sidenotes of the
  predecessor's Provenance layer. They are not paper margins.
* The *page margins* are the four edges of paper the content column stays
  inside, chosen by `MarginPreset` (narrow, normal, wide; 36, 72 and 108
  points today). The presets are uniform; the proportioned margins of the
  book page, narrower inside than outside and narrower at the head than the
  foot <<tschichold1991>> <<bringhurst2012>>, are a theme choice the
  preset vocabulary can grow to. The engine draws the running-head band inside the top margin
  and the footer band inside the bottom one.
* The *binding gutter* is extra width on the bound edge, so that the content
  clears the binding. It is a print-only setting, `BindingGutter`: *none*
  (symmetric margins, the universal output); *simplex*, extra width on the
  left edge of every page, for a single-sided printout bound on the left;
  *duplex*, extra width on the inside edge, mirrored between recto and verso,
  which is the `margin-inside` / `margin-outside` rule of the loose-leaf
  overlay <<csspage3>>. The gutter adds a fixed half inch to the preset's edge; the preset
  itself does not change.

The word *gutter* is reserved for the binding allowance.footnote:[The
distinction is this topic's. In bookwork the gutter is the inner margin of a
spread <<harts2014>>, and in the compositor's chase the gutter-stick is the
furniture between the pages <<moxon1683>>; both are the binding side, which
is why the word is kept for that side alone here and never for the screen's
margin regions.] The screen's margin tracks are *margin regions* and what
they hold is *marginalia*; the predecessor already uses those words, and the
family's `right-margin` keeps its name. When the Provenance layer prints, its marginalia go to the
*outside* page margin, the one away from the binding, so an identicon is
never swallowed by the gutter; the page margin for a printed Provenance layer
is therefore the wide preset at least, and the writer widens it if the
setting is narrower, reporting the change in the status line rather than
clipping the marks.

=== Keep and break rules

indexterm:[keep together, document surface]
indexterm:[page break, document surface]
Breaks are rules by block kind, not settings; the writer emits them as
Typst's `block(breakable:)` and keep settings. The rules a document adds:

* A heading keeps with the block that follows it, `keep-with-next` in XSL's
  words <<xsl11>>; a heading is never the last thing on a page.
* A paragraph breaks between lines, never within a line, and leaves at least
  two lines on each side of the break, the widow and orphan rule of the
  typographers <<bringhurst2012>> <<chicago2017>>. Since a line is a
  `StoredLine`, the break falls between source lines and provenance is
  undisturbed.
* A table keeps its caption and header row with the first body row and
  repeats the header row on each continuation page, as CSS lets a header
  row group repeat on paged media <<css21>>; it breaks between rows, and
  inside a cell only when one row exceeds a page (the Tables section).
* A listing breaks between lines like a paragraph, with no widow rule, so a
  long listing paginates rather than overflowing.
* A figure (a concept tree, a diagram, an image) is atomic and keeps with its
  caption; a figure taller than a band is scaled to the band, as the family's
  "fit" contract requires of a block that cannot fit its measure. A figure
  does not float: it prints where its row falls, and the keep rule moves it
  whole, which is the one of LaTeX's float placements <<lamport1994>> this
  surface keeps, since a floated figure would part from the provenance of
  the prose around it.
* A level-1 section starts per `SectionStart`: *flow* (no break), *new page*,
  or *recto* (new page, on a right-hand face, with a blank verso inserted),
  the last being the book convention for chapter openings <<chicago2017>>,
  required by the chapter-local folio scheme and the only value the
  loose-leaf binder accepts.
* A nested document starts as its outermost level-1 section would; it brings
  no page settings of its own (below).

=== Which settings are shared concepts

indexterm:[PrintSettings, shared concepts]
The existing enums are starter-set concepts with pinned public ids, persisted
by name in one `Encodable` record under `DocumentSurfaceSettingKeys`. The
document surface keeps every one of them and adds four. Nothing is renamed:
the constant names are the wire format.

.Settings concepts, existing and new
[cols="2,1,1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Concept | Exists | Used on screen | Note

| `PageSize`, `PageOrientation`
| yes
| Page layout only
| Unchanged. Reading has no page.

| `MarginPreset`
| yes
| Page layout only
| Unchanged. The screen's margin regions are grid tracks, not this.

| `DocumentTheme`
| yes
| both
| Face, base size and prose family. Reading draws prose in the theme too, so
the screen and the page differ in geometry and never in type.

| `FurnitureVisibility` (running head)
| yes
| both
| In Reading, hides the sticky header strip; in Page layout and print, the band.

| `PageNumberPlacement`
| yes
| Page layout only
| Gains `FOOTER_OUTSIDE`, the mirrored placement a duplex gutter needs.
Adding a constant is wire-compatible; renaming is not.

| `FolioScheme`
| new
| Page layout only
| continuous, chapter-local.

| `BindingGutter`
| new
| Page layout only
| none, simplex, duplex.

| `SectionStart`
| new
| both
| flow, new page, recto. In Reading a new-page or recto start draws as a rule
between sections, so the structure that will break a page is visible without
pages.

| `AsOfLine`
| new
| Page layout only
| shown, hidden; a `FurnitureVisibility` for the footer's as-of line, kept
as its own field so the running head's toggle stays what it is.
|===

The record grows from six fields to ten. Its encoder writes fields in order,
so the change is a new encoder version with the old six read and the four
new ones defaulted; the freeze test that pins the public ids extends to the
new concepts. The generated settings form of `KlPreferenceEditable` shows the
new fields with no further code, which is the reason to keep every choice an
enum rather than a number.

== The document in knowledge-layout terms

indexterm:[document surface, KlSupplementalArea]
indexterm:[area insertion, document surface]
The family says a surface is a block, so a surface can sit in another
surface's cell, and asserts that this is the composition the knowledge-layout
tier already performs when a host card holds grid-placed areas and an area
hosts a card. The assertion is right as an analogy and silent as a design: it
does not say which knowledge-layout type a document surface is, which type a
figure in it is, how a nested document's grid relates to its host's, who owns
the page settings when documents nest, or how expansion climbs through the
nesting. This section settles each.

=== The document is an area; a card hosts it

The document surface is an *area*, a `KlSupplementalArea` <<kl-area>>, as
`DocumentSurfaceArea` already is for the conversation's document: an area
with its own preferences node and lifecycle, implementing `KlExpandable` (its
expanded view is Page layout) and `KlPreferenceEditable` over `PrintSettings`. It
is never a card and never a window.

* A *card* hosts the document area. `StampedDocumentCard` today is an
  `AbstractHostCard` that draws its own content; it becomes a host card whose
  one area is the stamped document area, with the card's toolbar being the
  predecessor's contextual toolbar, and its Repository panel and History
  view (xref:dev-stamped-document-surface[], "The repository and its
  operations") as supplemental areas of the same card. `ClaudeCard` already holds a
  `DocumentSurfaceArea` card-internally; the same area class, restored from
  the card's preferences node, serves both, which is what "journals and git
  documents share one block model" means in layout terms.
* A *window* hosts cards: the journal card windows of
  xref:dev-kview-window-retirement[] remain, and a document card is one of
  them. The Scriptorium's retirement of the journal (xref:dev-scriptorium[])
  changes which window hosts the card, not that a card hosts the area.

=== Inserting a figure area into a document area

A document area is a `KlParent`: it has a `gridPaneForChildren()`, and that
grid pane *is* the twelve-track grid of the family, an `AreaGridSettings`
grid <<kl-area>> with twelve columns and one row per block. A figure in the document is a
*child area* of the document area, placed with the grid settings the layout
tier already has, where the family's named regions are the closed set of
column spans a child may take:

.Named regions as grid spans
[cols="1,1,2", options="header"]
|===
| Region | Columns | Child

| `body` | 3–10 | a prose block; a figure at the text measure
| `body-inset` | 4–9 | a narrower centred figure
| `left-half`, `right-half` | 3–6, 7–10 | two figures side by side on one row
| `full-bleed` | 1–12 | a wide table or a tree, across the margin regions
| `left-margin`, `right-margin` | 1–2, 11–12 | marginalia sharing a row with the body block
|===

A concept tree, a table of versions, or a diagram is thus a `KlArea` of the
kind the layout tier already produces for a field (`KlAreaForComponent`,
`KlAreaForList`, and the rest of `dev.ikm.komet.layout.area`), inserted as a
row with a span. Render dispatch (xref:dev-rich-render-dispatch[]) chooses the
area factory by block kind; this topic chooses only where the result sits.
The inserted area is a figure in the family's sense: it fits, it never
scrolls, and if it cannot fit its span it scales or promotes to `full-bleed`.
Its print form is the block's static adapter, scaled to the page's content
width by the same rule.

=== Inserting a document into a document, and into a card

indexterm:[nested document, document surface]
A document inserted into a document is a child document area placed in a row
of the host's grid, in one of the family's two embed modes:

* *Flow*: the nested document's rows dissolve into the host's. Its blocks are
  rows of the host's grid, its sections are sections of the host for the
  running head and the folio, and its grid tracks are not its own but the
  host's, measured to the host's `body`. This is what an AsciiDoc `include`
  is: the assembly that materialized thirty-six documents from one compendium
  is one host document with thirty-five flowed children, each still its own
  `StoredDocument` with its own lines and STAMPs.
* *Fit*: the nested document is a framed excerpt, bounded to a span, with a
  caption, as a figure. A quoted section of another document is this. It
  carries its own as-of coordinate in the caption, the family's "current as
  of" or "as authored" frame.

A document inserted into a card is the first case above with the card as
host: the card's `gridPaneForChildren()` holds the document area in a cell.
The card is not a document, so there is no flow mode at this level; the
document fits the cell and scrolls inside it as the family's one axis, which
is the only scroll there is.

=== Preferences and view context pass down

indexterm:[preferences, nested areas]
indexterm:[view context, nested areas]
Two things pass from a host to the areas inserted in it, and they pass by
different rules.

*Preferences* pass by nesting of nodes: a child area's preferences node is a
child of its host area's node, as `ClaudeCard` restores its
`DocumentSurfaceArea` from `preferences().node(DocumentSurfaceArea.class)`.
A figure area's settings live under the document's node, a flowed child
document's under its host's. Page settings are the exception and the rule
that follows from it: *the outermost document that is printed owns the page
settings*, and a flowed child contributes blocks, sections and keep rules
only. A child's own `PrintSettings`, if it has any from being opened alone,
are ignored while it is flowed. A fitted child, being a figure, has no page
settings at all.

*View context* passes by inheritance with a visible override, which is the
family's "coordinate-scope ambiguity" weak point answered. A figure area
resolves its content against the view coordinate of the document it sits in,
through the layout tier's context contract (`dev.ikm.komet.layout.context`),
unless the figure pins its own, as a frame "as authored" does. A pinned
figure shows a badge in its chrome naming the coordinate it is pinned to, so
that a tree governed by a different path than the prose around it is never
silent. A flowed child document inherits its host's coordinate and cannot
pin one; a fitted child may.

=== Expansion rungs through the nesting

indexterm:[expansion rung, document surface]
indexterm:[KlExpandable, document surface]
Expansion is the ladder of `dev.ikm.komet.layout.expand` <<kl-expand>>: a
`KlExpandable` figure fills the nearest `KlExpansionHost` rung, climbs one
rung per activation, and steps down on Escape. Hosts opt in by marking their pane with
a rung, which is how a window in `kview` is a rung without the layout tier
knowing it. The nesting of this topic gives the ladder its rungs:

. A figure area in a document expands to fill the *document area*, which
  `DocumentSurfaceArea` already marks as the rung named "Document".
. The document area expands to fill its *card*. Its expanded view is Page
  layout.
. The card expands to fill the *journal window*, and beyond that OS full
  screen, which a document allows (`osFullScreenCapable`) and a small table
  does not.

A flowed child document is *not* a rung: its blocks are the host's rows, and
a figure in it expands to the host document. A fitted child document is a
rung, named by its caption, since it is a place a reader recognises. The
preferences toggle of the expanded chrome, from `KlPreferenceEditable`, is
where the page settings of the section above are edited: expand the document
to Page layout, open preferences, and the generated form shows the ten fields.

== Print truth: Typst from the block model, settled

indexterm:[print truth, document surface]
indexterm:[WYSIWYG, document surface]
indexterm:[Typst, print target]
Three engines could lay the document into pages: the JavaFX engine, on
screen and through the printer from the running Komet; Prince, from HTML and
CSS Paged Media in the documentation pipeline <<prince-guide>>; and a
typesetter fed from the block model. The family topic names a fourth,
DocBook and XSL-FO <<walsh2010>> <<xsl11>>, as the print-target vocabulary,
which nothing in any chain renders. KEC settled the question twice on
2026-10-19, and the second decision supersedes the first.

=== The decision, and what changed

The morning's decision made the JavaFX engine the print truth, raised to
Prince's quality:

[quote, KEC, 2026-10-19 (superseded the same day)]
____
The goal is to be able to render print at equivalent resolution/quality as
prince, but to do it within JavaFX, and not depend on prince as part of the
print chain. In some cases, we may render from FX controls, in ways that
prince may not be able to directly manage. And also, there maybe
html/css/prince limitations that we want to step around.
____

Later the same day KEC revised it, leaning first ("I'm leaning toward Typst
as the final target in the chain. And I'm hoping that we can render Java FX
on the screen with excellent fidelity.") and then settling:

[quote, KEC, 2026-10-19 (settled)]
____
Typst is the final target in the chain, written from the block model and
never hand-authored; the Flow view stays FX-native; the Pages view renders
Typst output; diagrams reach Typst as SVG or via its Graphviz plugin; the
four AsciiDoc-source disciplines apply.
____

What stayed: no Prince anywhere in the chain, and the print chain inside
Komet. What changed: the typesetting is Typst's <<maedje2022>>, a
typesetter built for the purpose, not a typesetter written in JavaFX over
the toolkit's text layout. The JavaFX engine's paged preview, which the
morning's decision would have grown into a typesetter, is not that
typesetter; it lays out the Page layout view over the shared page model,
and print is Typst's output.

On 2026-10-20, after trying the built surface, KEC settled the on-screen
side: "I'd like to have the JavaFX page layout (as close to perfect typist
rendering as possible) to be a view mode, so we can have 'source',
'reading', and 'page layout' as views." So the Page layout view is JavaFX's
over the shared page model, tracking Typst as closely as JavaFX allows
(the views section), and the 2026-10-19 statement that the on-screen pages
render Typst's PNG or SVG is superseded: that rendering is an option for a
later exact-preview mode, not the view. Minutes later KEC refined it,
"Can't you use Typst to retrofit line breaks?", which makes Typst the
layout oracle of a two-pass Page layout. Print and PDF stay Typst's.

=== One author of Typst: the writer

indexterm:[Typst writer, block model]
A *Typst writer* reads the block model and emits Typst source. It is the
only author of Typst: no Typst is hand-written, none is committed, and git
holds AsciiDoc and nothing else. Typst and the Tinkar graph are both
projections of the AsciiDoc source, the one for the page and the other for
provenance, and the writer takes the graph's blocks, with their identities,
as its input, so that what prints is what the surface shows and selects.
The writer keeps every emitted span aligned to the block, row, cell or line
it came from, which is what the exact-preview mapping (below), the PDF's
way back to a block and the jump-from-click of Typst's introspection depend
on.

=== Reading and Page layout are FX-native; print is Typst's

The *Reading* view is the FX-native surface, unchanged from the predecessor:
`StampedDocumentCard`'s runs carrying their `StoredLine`s, hover provenance,
selection by line, and the editing in place of the section below. KEC's hope
for it is fidelity, that FX renders the same type with the same metrics as
the page (fonts, below), not that FX paginates.

The *Page layout* view is FX-native too, laid out by the paged engine from
the stored blocks over the shared page model, in two passes with Typst as
the layout oracle (the views section above), and it is the on-screen page.
Print and PDF are Typst's output, written from the block model by the
writer. KEC settled this on 2026-10-20, and it supersedes the statement of
2026-10-19 that the on-screen pages render Typst's per-page PNG or SVG;
that rendering becomes an option for a later *exact-preview mode* rather
than the view, and Typst's compile serves the view as its oracle instead. Where it is offered, a click on
Typst's page maps back to a source span through Typst's introspection, the
`jump_from_click` of `typst-ide` <<typst-ide>>, and from the span, through
the writer's alignment, to the block or line; the same alignment lets a
reader of the PDF be taken to the block in Komet. Typst's own `query` and
`context` give the writer the page of any block, so the Reading view's
position marker can say which printed page a line falls on, and the
fidelity measure can compare that page with the Page layout view's.

=== Embedding is a spike, not a given

indexterm:[typst-cffi, embedding]
The Typst compiler is Rust. A thin C interface exists, the `typst-cffi`
crate <<typst-cffi>>, which tracks Typst 0.15.1 <<typst-0.15>> and exposes
compile-to-PDF, PNG and SVG; no Java binding exists. Embedding it in Komet
means binding through the JDK's foreign-function and memory API, a native
library per platform carried in the jlink image, and the spike decides
whether that holds: whether the library builds and loads on each platform,
whether compile latency serves pass two of the Page layout view after an
edit, and whether the layout frames with their source spans, the oracle's
exact route, and the introspection an exact-preview click needs are
reachable through the C surface or need a wider one.

The *headless target* needs no embedding: the writer emits Typst and the
Typst command line compiles it. That target comes first, and it replaces
Prince in the documentation toolchain and in CI for the documents the writer
covers; in Komet the same binary is bundled in the jlink image and run as a
process until the spike settles (the pipeline section below), at the cost
of a process per compile.

=== Fonts

The same families are embedded by Typst and loaded by FX: the Noto families,
chosen for coverage and for being free to embed, so that Reading, Page
layout and the Typst page share glyph metrics at the same sizes, which is
what lets pass two of Page layout place Typst's lines in JavaFX's hand
without a glyph moving. Typst hyphenates from the document's language
patterns, breaks paragraphs optimally and hangs punctuation into the margin
by default (`hyphenate`, `lang`, `overhang` <<typst-text>>); pass one
breaks greedily and does none of those, and pass two retrofits all three
from the oracle.

=== Diagrams

indexterm:[diagrams, Typst]
Typst embeds SVG and PNG natively <<typst-0.14>>. Every diagram reaches it
as one or the other, with no server and no native binary:

* Graphviz renders in-document through the `diagraph` package, Graphviz
  compiled to WebAssembly as a Typst plugin <<diagraph>>; the writer emits
  the DOT source and Typst lays it out.
* PlantUML runs in-process in Komet in its pure-Java layout mode, Smetana
  <<plantuml-smetana>>, and hands the writer SVG; no Graphviz binary is
  required.
* Komet's own renderers, the koncept tree and the graph figure, export SVG
  of what they draw, so a live FX control prints as a vector of itself.
* Diagram text travels as paths, or in fonts Typst can find, so a label is
  never substituted.
* No Kroki server, no Graphviz installation, no network.

=== The four AsciiDoc-source disciplines

indexterm:[AsciiDoc, source disciplines]
Because git holds AsciiDoc alone, the source must stay AsciiDoc that every
reader can render. Four disciplines hold.

. *Standard AsciiDoc wherever it has the semantics.* Tables, footnotes,
  cross references, index terms, bibliography and citations, and `stem`
  blocks are written as Asciidoctor defines them <<asciidoctor-tables>>
  <<asciidoctor-footnotes>>; the writer maps each to Typst, and LaTeX math
  in a `stem` block reaches Typst through `mitex` <<mitex>>.
. *Extend only where AsciiDoc has nothing*, through named macros or tagged
  blocks, each with a legible fallback in plain Asciidoctor, on GitHub and
  under Prawn: a Koncept badge falls back to its label, a live table to a
  static table. Never a passthrough block, never a backend conditional in a
  topic.
. *The dialect parser types every extension block*, so that provenance and
  editing do not stop at the edge of a macro: a live table is a typed block
  with cells, not an opaque one.
. *The IKE-DOC backend matrix is the test* <<ike-doc>>: a construct that
  renders under html5, Prawn and the Typst target, or has a stated fallback
  under each, is allowed; one that renders under Typst alone is not.

=== What Typst gives, against the capability list

The morning's decision listed what a typesetter must own. Typst owns most of
it already; the table says how, and what it costs.

.The capability list, met by Typst
[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Capability | How Typst meets it

| Typesetting
| Optimized line breaking in the Knuth and Plass line <<knuthplass1981>>,
hyphenation from language patterns <<liang1983>> selected by `lang`, and
optical margin alignment through `overhang` <<typst-text>>, the protrusion
of microtype <<thanh2000>>; widows and orphans by the paragraph's own
settings. Prose keeps its `StoredLine`s because the writer emits one span
per line and Typst's breaking is within the span.

| Footnotes and sidenotes
| A native `footnote`, placed at the foot of the page its reference falls on
and continued across pages, the footnote of the printed book
<<chicago2017>> rather than Asciidoctor's endnote <<asciidoctor-footnotes>>;
a sidenote by placement in the outside margin beside its reference, where
the Provenance layer's marginalia also go, clear of the gutter.

| Running heads from section text
| Context-driven: a header that queries the headings before the current page
and sets the verso title and the recto section, which is `string-set` done
in a language <<cssgcpm3>> rather than a stylesheet.

| Cross references with page numbers
| `ref` with the page counter in context; Typst iterates layout to a fixed
point itself, so the two passes LaTeX runs by hand <<lamport1994>> are the
compiler's, and the writer emits a reference and nothing more.

| Index and generated lists
| Outlines by kind: `outline(target: figure.where(kind: table))` is the list
of tables, and the same for figures and equations <<typst-outline>>, the
`lot` of DocBook <<stayton2007>> <<stayton2016>> as a query; the index from
queried index entries with their pages, in the document's folio scheme.

| Tables
| Native tables with repeating multi-level headers (`table.header`), footers
and row and column spans <<typst-outline>>; the Tables section's
requirements map onto them, with continued captions from context.

| Mathematics and bibliography
| Native math, with LaTeX through `mitex` <<mitex>>; bibliographies through
CSL with a hundred shipped styles and custom `.csl` files <<typst-0.14>>.

| Output
| Tagged PDF by default and PDF/UA-1 optional since 0.14, with PDF/UA-2
pending, and PDF/A at every level <<typst-0.14>> <<typst-blog-0.14>>
<<iso32000>> <<iso19005>> <<iso14289>>; PNG and SVG per page for the
exact-preview mode and for the fidelity measure. The `PrinterJob` path prints the PDF through the operating system's
dialog.
|===

The costs are four. A native dependency, if the embedding spike succeeds,
and a process per compile if it does not. A two-model mapping, the block
model to Typst and Typst's spans back to blocks, which the writer must keep
exact or an exact-preview click lands on the wrong line. Live FX controls
print as exported SVG, a vector of the control at the moment of export,
never the control itself. And a young project's roadmap: Typst is at 0.15
<<typst-0.15>>, its PDF/UA-2 is pending, and its C interface is one
person's crate; the disciplines above keep the source independent of it, so
a change of target is a new writer, not a new corpus.

The Prince topics, xref:dev-pdf-loose-leaf-variant[] and
xref:dev-loose-leaf-index-and-diff[], remain the record of the paper
conventions, the folio schemes, the duplex gutter, the chapter-local index;
the headless Typst target takes over their rendering as the writer covers
the Maven-built books.

== Typst in the documentation pipeline and in Komet

indexterm:[Typst, documentation pipeline]
indexterm:[ike-doc-maven-plugin, Typst renderer]
Typst reaches the page from two front ends, the Maven-built books and
Komet, and the two must agree. This section slots Typst into the pipeline
where Prince sits today, puts the first Typst into Komet without a line of
native code, and gives both front ends one writer.

=== How Prince is slotted today

The pipeline is `ike-doc-maven-plugin` (`ike-docs`, package
`network.ike.docs.plugin`) <<ike-doc-plugin>>. Its `Backend` enum names
three Asciidoctor backends, `HTML("html5", "html")`, `PDF("pdf",
"pdf-prawn")` and `DOCBOOK("docbook5", "docbook")`, and `AsciidocMojo`
converts each enabled one. `RenderPdfMojo` takes a `renderer` (prince, ah,
weasyprint, xep, fop), an input and an output directory and a stylesheet,
and runs the external executable: the CSS renderers over Asciidoctor's print
HTML with `ike-print.css`, the FO renderers over DocBook. 
`PrepareRendererOutputMojo` carries one `ike.skip.<renderer>` flag per
renderer (`ike.skip.prince` false by default, the others true) and makes the
`pdf-<renderer>/` directory for each that runs; `CopyDefaultPdfMojo` copies
the `ike.pdf.default` renderer's output, Prince by default, to `pdf/`; and
`ike-doc-resources` carries one assembly descriptor per renderer, with
`assembly/prince.xml` zipping `pdf-prince/`. Prince never touched AsciiDoc:
it consumed HTML. Typst consumes neither HTML nor DocBook, so it enters at
the backend, not at the renderer alone.

=== The converter: a `typst` backend

indexterm:[AsciidoctorJ converter, typst backend]
An AsciidoctorJ converter in Java, registered for the backend name `typst`
through `@ConverterFor` and the instance's `javaConverterRegistry`
<<asciidoctorj-converter>>, walks the Asciidoctor AST and emits `.typ`.
`Backend` gains `TYPST("typst", "typst")`, and `AsciidocMojo` converts it
beside the three it has, under an `ike.skip.typst` of its own. The converter
lives in `ike-docs` beside `koncept-asciidoc-extension`, so that the Koncept
macros and the converter version together. It emits standard Typst for what
AsciiDoc has, tables, footnotes, cross references, index terms, bibliography
and `stem`, and it emits nothing it cannot read back: the four source
disciplines of the print-truth section hold for the pipeline as for Komet.

=== The renderer: `typst compile` into `pdf-typst/`

A `typst` renderer joins `RenderPdfMojo`'s `RendererType` with `.typ` as its
input extension, running `typst compile` with `--root` at the document's
source root (so `image` and `include` paths resolve) and `--font-path` at
the pipeline's font directory (the Noto families of the print-truth
section) <<typst-cli>>, into `pdf-typst/`. `PrepareRendererOutputMojo`
gains `ike.skip.typst`; `ike-doc-resources` gains `assembly/typst.xml`
zipping `pdf-typst/`; and `ike.pdf.default` accepts `typst`, so the copy to
`pdf/` can take Typst's output once the acceptance test passes. The Typst
command line is a single static binary, installed through Homebrew on `kec`
and on the `ikes` TeamCity agent, pinned to one Typst version in the
parent's properties as the renderer executables are today; the renderer
refuses a binary of another version rather than rendering with it, since
0.x minors break (the comparison below).

=== The Koncept extension's Typst path

indexterm:[Koncept extension, Typst]
The Koncept extension renders a badge three ways today, as inline SVG for
the html5 family, as a file-referenced image for DocBook and Prawn, and as
a phrase where an image cannot go. It gains a fourth, Typst: a badge *drawn
natively* in Typst from the appearance spec, the pill, pads, radius, colours,
label face and stamp pentagon transcribed once into a shared `koncept.typ`
template, with the identicon written beside the document as a
content-addressed PNG and placed with pixelated scaling, as the DocBook and
Prawn path already writes it. The badge demo of 2026-10-19 settled this
against embedding the renderer's SVG <<typst-badge-demo>>: Typst substitutes
a font inside an SVG silently, with no warning, so today's `sans-serif`
label and the spec's family string both fell back to Libertinus Serif; an
embedded SVG badge cannot adapt to a narrow table cell, where it is cropped,
shrunk to illegibility or stretched, while a native badge wraps its label
inside the pill; and an SVG badge sized to the spec pushes the lines that
carry it apart, while the native box paints outside its layout box and
leaves the leading alone. Native text in Typst does warn of a missing font,
which is the failure mode we want. One badge in the compendium and one in
Komet's printout are then one template call, not one picture. The glossary
the extension assembles is emitted by the converter itself, from the tree
processor's collected entries, with no Asciidoctor postprocessor in the
path. That matters for a reason the code records: an SPI-registered
postprocessor crashes the Prawn backend, which is why the extension's
paragraph clean-up is guarded today, and the Typst path has no such
postprocessor to guard.

=== In Komet: the bundled binary first

indexterm:[Komet, bundled Typst binary]
Komet's first step is the same binary, bundled in the jlink image and run as
a process: the block-model writer emits `.typ`, Komet runs `typst compile`
with `--format pdf` for print, and `svg` or `png` at the display's `--ppi`
for the fidelity measure and the exact-preview mode, and reads the files
back. Zero foreign-function code, one
binary per platform in the image, the same pinned version as the pipeline.
The embedding spike of the print-truth section follows, for what a process
cannot give: incremental recompile after an edit and click-to-source
through `typst-ide`'s introspection.

=== One shared Typst writer library

indexterm:[Typst writer library, ike-docs]
One Java library in `ike-docs` holds the Typst document model, the page and
furniture setup, and the serializer to `.typ`. Two front ends feed it: the
converter, from the Asciidoctor AST, and `komet-claude-plugin`'s block-model
writer, which consumes the library the way it consumes `stamp-adoc-core`
today, as a versioned dependency. The compendium and the Komet printout then
agree by construction, because one serializer writes both, and the Page
terminology's concepts drive that one serializer: a `FolioScheme` chosen in
Komet and a `:folio-scheme:` attribute in a topic reach the same page setup.
What differs between the front ends is only what the model is built from.

=== The acceptance test

The test is the ANF compendium (`knowledge-kompendium/anf`), rendered with
Prince and with Typst and compared page by page: the same breaks, the same
running heads and folios, tables of 49 rows crossing pages with their
headers repeated, the 109 tables at their `cols` proportions, footnotes
placed, the index and the lists generated. Differences are read against
the comparison below and settled one by one; the copy to `pdf/` switches to
Typst when none remains that a reader would notice.

The test carries a *scale gate* as well. Typst lays out the whole document
and re-lays it out until page numbers, counters and running heads stop
changing, up to five passes, and only then exports pages; page-at-a-time is
an export choice, never a layout one, which is what makes the furniture and
the cross references possible at all <<typst-convergence>>. Field reports
put the native compiler well inside our sizes: a table-heavy book of over
two thousand pages in about a minute, memory growing linearly with content,
with the known cost being `context` evaluated in every page's header and a
regression in the convergence check in 0.15 on a 300-page document
<<typst-scale>>. So the gate is measured, not assumed: the ANF compendium
and the full lab compendium compiled with `--timings`, recording wall time
and peak memory, against a budget set before the copy switches; and the
writer keeps the running head to one cheap query per page, avoids chains
where one element's placement depends on another's measured position, and
emits one file per chapter. The Typst web application is not in the chain
and is no measure of it: it runs the same compiler as WebAssembly under the
browser's memory ceiling, at a fraction of native speed.

*Prior art.* Two bridges from AsciiDoc to Typst exist, neither
production-grade and neither in Java: a TypeScript converter over the
Asciidoctor core library that walks the parsed AST <<asciidoctor-typst>>,
whose construct mapping ours mirrors, and a shell script chaining
Asciidoctor to DocBook and Pandoc's Typst writer <<adoc2typ>>, Pandoc
having no AsciiDoc reader. Neither carries identities per block and line or
the Koncept extensions, which is why the converter is written rather than
adopted; the DocBook route serves as a baseline to compare output against.

=== First evidence

indexterm:[Typst, pipeline spike]
The converter and the renderer were spiked on 2026-10-19
<<typst-pipeline-spike>>. A new reactor subproject,
`typst-asciidoc-converter`, holds the seed of the shared writer library
(package `network.ike.docs.typst`: text escaping and labels, a page and
furniture preamble) with the AsciidoctorJ converter as its first front end
in a sub-package; `Backend.TYPST`, `ike.skip.typst` defaulting to true
everywhere so nothing changes by default, `RendererType.TYPST` with an
`ike.typst.version` pin that refuses a binary of another version,
`assembly/typst.xml`, and a `-Dike.pdf.typst` profile. Three topics rendered
end to end with no Typst warning and no fallback block: a design topic with
four tables, an ANF topic with fifteen tables, and a regulatory topic with
three tables and 49 footnotes, each placed at the foot of its page; Typst
compiled them in 54 to 152 milliseconds to PDF and under a quarter of a
second to PNG, with AsciidoctorJ's start-up dominating at about 1.6
seconds, paid once per Maven execution. Twenty-eight converter unit tests,
one per construct, pass.

What the spike did not do is what the writer library is for: the page
setup is one static preamble with no section numbering, table of contents,
index, list of tables, chapter-local folios or `SectionStart` rules; keep
rules are Typst's defaults; the Koncept badge is a placeholder and the
glossary is not emitted. Two operational facts for the pipeline: `mitex`
0.2.5 fails under Typst 0.15.1 (a symbol 0.15 removed), so 0.2.7 is pinned;
and Typst fetches packages from its registry on first use and caches them
under the user's cache directory, so a CI agent needs network once or a
seeded cache. Whether the `minimal-fonts` subset carries the serif family
the preamble names was not checked; a missing family falls back silently to
the next in the list.

=== Typst against Prince

indexterm:[Typst, versus Prince]
The comparison KEC was given, kept here so the choice is made with its
costs in view. *Typst is ahead* on optimized line breaking, hyphenation and
optical margins <<typst-text>>, native mathematics, openness (an open
compiler, no license, no watermark) and speed. *Prince is ahead* on the
maturity of CSS Paged Media, page floats, margin boxes, running elements
and multi-column balancing <<prince-guide>>; on PDF/X and ICC colour
management; on vertical CJK and broad internationalisation; and on
stability, where Typst at 0.x still breaks between minors <<typst-0.15>>.
*They are even* on SVG, on fonts, on tables since 0.14, and on PDF/UA-1
<<typst-0.14>>. None of Prince's leads touches what the corpus prints
today; its stability lead is why the version is pinned and the binary is
bundled rather than taken from the path.

== Tables

indexterm:[tables, document surface]
indexterm:[table, cells as blocks]
Tables are not a block kind among others on this surface; they are the block
kind the corpus is made of. The ANF document alone has 109 tables across its
35 topics, every one with a header row, almost all of two or three columns
with proportional widths (`[cols="2,3", options="header"]`
<<asciidoctor-tables>>), some 1,400 cells
whose text runs onto continuation lines, and tables of up to 49 rows that
will cross a page however it is set. What the surface does with a table
today is the weakest part of it, at every layer.

* The dialect parser (`stamp-adoc-explorer` core,
  `IkeDialectParser.parseRows`) models a row as a group of lines separated by
  blank lines and says in so many words that cells are not modelled yet. A
  cell therefore has no identity: no `StoredLine` of its own, no STAMP, and
  nothing an edit or an annotation can name.
* `StampedDocumentCard` renders a row by joining its lines and splitting on
  the bar into `Label`s in a `GridPane`, ignoring the `cols` widths and every
  span.
* `PagedDocumentEngine` treats a table as atomic: a table taller than a page
  is pushed to a page top and then straddles the break, with no header
  repeated and no continued caption.

The comparison that sets the bar is the two print chains the corpus has
known. Prince over html5 gives fixed percentage column widths from a
`colgroup`, weak row splitting at a tall cell, no footnotes inside a table,
no continued caption, and no fit-to-width or automatic landscape
<<prince-guide>>. DocBook's CALS table model has `colspec`, `spanspec`,
`thead` and `tfoot` <<cals1995>> <<walsh2010>>, and the Informatics
customization Stayton wrote for the ANF ballot documents turned hyphenation
off inside tables and started chapters on an odd page <<stayton2016>>; the
stock stylesheets do neither <<stayton2007>>. Typst, written from the block
model (the print-truth section), is bounded by neither, and KEC's
requirements for the table are these eight.

.Table requirements
[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Requirement | What it means

| 1. Cells as blocks
| *Parser work, in the dialect parser.* A cell becomes a block with its own
identity, the child of its row, the row the child of its table, so that the
cell is what provenance, editing and action subjects reach: a cell has the
`StoredLine`s of its text, its introducing STAMP, and is the subject of
`Edit document block` and of an annotation. An AsciiDoc-style cell (cell style `a`) holds
nested blocks, paragraphs, lists and listings, which are children of the
cell block; a plain cell holds runs. The row stays as the grouping the
parser has, now of cell blocks rather than of lines; the table block carries
the `cols` spec, the options and the caption. Everything below assumes
this; nothing below is possible without it.

| 2. Column widths
| Each column's *min-content* width (its widest unbreakable word or figure)
and *max-content* width (its widest cell set on one line) are measured, the
two bounds of automatic table layout <<css21>>, and the `cols` proportions
are the weights with which the slack between the sum of the minima and the
available measure is distributed; a column never drops below its minimum.
Rules are set as booktabs sets them, above the head, under it and below the
last row, none between columns <<booktabs>>. The table is fitted to `body` first, to `full-bleed`
if the minima exceed the body's measure, and to a landscape page last, only
when the minima exceed the full-bleed measure of a portrait page. Spans take
the summed width of the columns they cover and contribute their minimum
divided across them.

| 3. Row breaking
| A table breaks between rows by default, and inside a tall cell only when
one row by itself exceeds a page, the rule Prince applies weakly and CALS did
not have. Header rows repeat at the top of every continuation page and the
continuation carries the caption again with "(continued)", as `longtable`
repeats its head and continued caption <<longtable>> and as XSL places a
table marker in the repeated header <<xsl11>>; the running head of a page
whose content is wholly a continued table names the table. Hyphenation is
off inside tables, as the Informatics customization set it <<stayton2016>>,
because a hyphenated word in a narrow cell reads worse than a ragged one.

| 4. Keep rules by size
| A small table, one that fits in a band with its caption, keeps together as
a figure does; a large one flows across pages under the breaking rules
above. The threshold is the band, not a row count, so the same table keeps
on one page size and flows on another; the caption keeps with the first
rows and the last row keeps with at least one other, so a table never leaves
a lone row on a page.

| 5. Table notes
| A footnote whose reference is inside a table is a *table note*, placed
under the table rather than at the foot of the page, numbered per table with
its own marks (letters, so they do not collide with the page's footnotes, as
the style manuals set them <<chicago2017>>),
and repeated under the last part of a table that continues. This is the
footnote inside a table that Prince cannot place.

| 6. Numbering and the list of tables
| A captioned table is numbered by chapter, `Table 3-2` under the
chapter-local scheme and `Table 12` under the continuous one, from the
table's typed block, and the list of tables of the print-truth section is
generated from those typed blocks with their folios, the DocBook `lot`
<<stayton2007>> KEC used to append by hand.

| 7. Live tables
| A table from the knowledge base, a pattern table through the Koncept
`pattern-table` macro, is a `TableView` on screen, a live control over the
view coordinate. It lays out through the *same table block*: the control's
columns and rows are presented to the width algorithm, the breaking rules
and the numbering as cells, so it prints as a typeset table with its header
repeated and its notes placed, never as a snapshot of the control. The
as-of coordinate the table resolved is its caption's frame, as the family
asks of every live figure.

| 8. The expansion figure on screen
| On screen a table is the family's expansion figure <<kl-expand>>: it snaps
to `body`, or
to `full-bleed` when its minima exceed the body's measure, and when its
minima exceed the card's width it does not scroll sideways, which the family
forbids, but climbs a rung of the expansion ladder, to the document, then
the card, then the window, where the width algorithm runs again against the
wider measure. A live table expanded is still live; a stored table expanded
is still selectable by cell.
|===

The parser work of the first requirement is the one named dependency outside
Komet: the dialect parser is `stamp-adoc-explorer`'s, deployed as the
snapshot the assistant plugin consumes, so cells as blocks land there first
and the card follows.

== Editing in place, within limits

indexterm:[edit in place, document surface]
indexterm:[line provenance, surviving an edit]
WYSIWYG editing on this surface means editing a block where it is read, in
Reading, in the theme the page will print in, and seeing the result in Page
layout.
It does not mean editing everything, and the limits are chosen so that
provenance by line survives every edit the surface allows.

.What is edited in place, and what is not
[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Edited in place | How

| Prose blocks: paragraph, heading text, list item, admonition body
| The predecessor's `Edit block`: the block flips to its edit projection
(below), the person edits text, and the result lands as an uncommitted
`Edit document block` version under the person's, or the assistant's, author
concept, settled by the git commit (xref:dev-komet-actions[]).

| Table rows and cells
| Once cells are blocks (the Tables section), a cell is edited as a block
and is the subject of its own edit; a row is edited as a row when the cells
of one row flip to edit projections together, and the result is one edit per
cell changed. Adding or removing a row is a table edit with a different row
count. Until the parser models cells, a row is edited as its lines in Source
view.

| Not edited in place | Why, and where instead

| Furniture
| Derived from structure and settings. Change the setting in the preferences
form, or change the heading the running head is derived from.

| Cross-document references
| An `xref` resolves to another document's anchor; the text under it is the
target's, and editing it is editing the target. Follow the reference and
edit there. The reference macro itself is text of the referring line and
edits as such in Source view.

| Includes
| An included file is a flowed child document, its own `StoredDocument` with
its own lines. A block in it is edited as a block of that document, under
its author and settled by a commit to that file, while the host shows it in
place. The `include` line of the host is edited in Source view as text.

| Block attributes, roles and options
| Source view, as text. They are structure, not prose, and the Reading view
does not show them.

| Figures from the store
| A tree or a diagram edits through its own editor, the structured block's
projection flip of xref:dev-rich-interaction-surface[]; nothing in the
document holds its content.

| Marginalia
| Never edited. An identicon is a fact and a review mark is an act of
xref:dev-stamp-annotation[annotation] made through the context menu.
|===

=== Provenance across an edit

An edit is a new version of one block. The block's text is split into lines
again, and each new line is matched to the old lines of the block by content:
a line whose text is unchanged keeps its `StoredLine`, its introducing STAMP
and its annotations; a changed or added line gets a new `StoredLine` whose
STAMP is the uncommitted edit's, with the editor as author; a removed line is
gone from the block and keeps its history in the store. The match is by the
same per-line content hash the annotation trailer uses
(xref:dev-annotation-trailer[], `path:from-to@rev#hash`), so a line the
trailer can name after the edit is the line it named before. An annotation on
a changed line is in force on the old line, which the Reading view no longer
shows; the predecessor's review-queue mark then shows the block as holding an
annotation on a line that moved on, which is the staleness rule of
xref:dev-stamp-annotation[] made visible. When the person's commit settles
the edit, materialization reads the committed file and the new lines receive
the commit's STAMP in place of the edit's; cancellation and restore follow
xref:dev-komet-actions[] unchanged.

=== Provenance across a reflow

A page is a view, never a record. Pagination is Typst's function of the
emitted blocks and the page settings, and nothing in the store refers to a
page. A
change of page size, margins, gutter or theme, or an edit that lengthens a
paragraph, moves blocks between pages and touches no provenance, because
provenance is on lines and blocks. It follows that a folio is never a
provenance anchor: an annotation names lines, a citation of a printed page
is resolved through the as-of line to the commit and then to the lines, and
the loose-leaf index locates by chapter-local folio only because that scheme
is stable across the reprint of other chapters
(xref:dev-loose-leaf-index-and-diff[]). The Page layout view re-lays out
after an edit in Reading at once, in pass one, and settles to Typst's lines
in pass two a moment later; a person editing a heading sees the running
head change on the facing Page layout view, which is the one WYSIWYG
feedback that reflow makes cheap. The latency of pass two is what the
embedding spike measures.

== The prose control, reconciled

indexterm:[prose control, TextFlow versus RichTextArea]
Three topics name a prose control and seem to disagree.
xref:dev-rich-interaction-surface[The rich interaction surface] says prose
blocks are `RichTextArea`, and the conversation's `DocumentSurface` draws them
so today, view-only and sized to content. xref:dev-stamped-document-surface[The
stamped document surface] draws a paragraph as a `TextFlow` of runs, one per
source line, and leaves `TextFlow` versus `RichTextArea` as its first
decision. The family says only that prose is a small per-block control.

The reconciliation is that the two topics describe two *projections* of one
block, and the projection flip of the rich interaction surface is per block,
so the read projection and the edit projection need not be the same control.

* The *read projection* of a prose block is the `TextFlow` of runs. It is
  what provenance by line needs, it carries no model of its own to keep in
  step with the store, and it owns no scrollbar. Print no longer depends on
  the control at all: the Typst writer reads the block model, so the read
  control is free to be whatever reading needs.
* The *edit projection* is the `RichTextArea`, one per block while that block
  is being edited, with the text and `k:` tokens the rich interaction surface
  specifies; on commit or cancel the block returns to its runs. A block edits
  as the rich interaction surface says and reads as the stamped document
  surface says.

This resolves the predecessor's first decision in favour of `TextFlow` for
reading without giving up the `RichTextArea`, and it asks one sentence of
xref:dev-rich-interaction-surface[]: that "prose blocks are `RichTextArea`"
be read as "prose blocks *edit* as `RichTextArea`". The conversation's
`DocumentSurface` then moves its read projection to runs when the journal and
the git document share the one block model, which the predecessor already
expects.

== Terminology

indexterm:[document surface, terminology]

Everything above names things. Where a name is a starter-set concept, a

of that holds. This section names the terminology the design needs and does
not yet have, grounded in what `ike-starter-set` declares today
(`ike-terms`, package `network.ike.foundation.ike.terms`), and records the

=== Tables terminology

indexterm:[terminology, tables]
indexterm:[CALS, table terminology]
The table terminology adopts the OASIS CALS exchange table model
<<cals1995>> rather than inventing names: it is the model DocBook tables
are written in <<walsh2010>>, the model XSL formats <<xsl11>>, and the one
the pipeline's own stylesheets once rendered. Each CALS element or attribute
becomes a concept under a *Table model* root in the foundation module, and
the writer maps each to Typst's table, following CALS and XSL table
formatting <<xsl11>> for what the concept means on the page and CSS 2.1's
automatic layout for widths <<css21>>.

.Table terminology, after CALS
[cols="1,1,2", options="header"]
|===
| Concept | CALS | What it holds

| Table group | `tgroup` | The columns, spans, head, body and foot of one table; a block's child, so a table with two groups is allowed as CALS allows it.
| Column spec | `colspec` | Column name and number; *column width*, proportional (`2*`, the `cols` proportion) or fixed (a length); alignment, character alignment and separators as defaults for the column.
| Span spec | `spanspec` | A named span from a start column to an end column (`namest`, `nameend`).
| Table head, body, foot | `thead`, `tbody`, `tfoot` | The row groups; the head repeats on continuation, the foot closes each part.
| Table entry | `entry` | The cell: *column start* and *column end* (`namest`, `nameend`) or a span name; *more rows* (`morerows`) for a row span; *alignment* (left, centre, right, justify, char) and *character alignment* (`char`, `charoff`); *vertical alignment*; and its members, the lines or nested blocks.
| Page-wide | `pgwide` | Whether the table takes the full measure, the family's `full-bleed`.
| Orientation | `orient` | Portrait or landscape for this table, the width algorithm's last fit.
| Frame and separators | `frame`, `colsep`, `rowsep` | The rules drawn, which booktabs reduces to three.
|===

The *Document table cell* kind already exists in the structure set; what is
missing is the parser emitting it and the entry's attributes having
somewhere to live. Today every CALS attribute of an AsciiDoc table lives
only in the raw parameters string of the table block
(`[cols="2,3", options="header"]`, a cell's `2+|` or `.2+|` prefix). The
patterns proposed: a *Table group pattern* (column specs, span specs, head,
body, foot, page-wide, orientation, frame) attached to the table block; a
*Column spec pattern*; and a *Table entry pattern* (column start, column
end, more rows, alignment, character alignment, vertical alignment) attached
to the cell block, whose lines stay in the structure pattern's members.

Two names in the Tables section are this topic's own and are marked so: the
*live table binding*, a pattern that binds a table block to the knowledge-base
pattern or query it is drawn from and to the coordinate frame it resolved
(the family's "current as of" or "as authored"), which CALS has no notion
of; and *cell-level provenance*, the cell as a block carrying its own
`StoredLine`s and STAMPs, which no table model carries because no table
model is materialized from git.

=== Page terminology

indexterm:[terminology, page]
indexterm:[PrintSettings, starter-set concepts]
The page terminology names what the furniture section chooses. A *Page
model* root in the foundation module holds *page size* (Letter, Legal and
the ISO sizes), *page orientation* (portrait, landscape), *margin preset*
(narrow, normal, wide), *binding gutter* (none, simplex, duplex), *running
head content* (document title, current section, title and section),
*running footer content* (folio, as-of line), *folio placement* (none,
header right, footer centre, footer right, footer outside), *folio scheme*
(continuous, chapter-local), *section start* (flow, new page, recto), the
*keep rules* (keep with next, keep together, widow and orphan lines),
*furniture visibility* (shown, hidden) and *document theme* (default,
manuscript). A *Page settings pattern* with one field per choice is the
semantic form, so that a document's page settings can version with the
document as a layout chronology of xref:dev-rich-interaction-surface[]
does; the plugin's preferences node remains the per-machine cache of the
same value.

Six of these already have identities. The plugin's print-settings enums
(`PageSize`, `PageOrientation`, `MarginPreset`, `DocumentTheme`,
`FurnitureVisibility`, `PageNumberPlacement`) pin a public id on every
constant inside `komet-claude-plugin`, with the comment that each "can
become a Tinkar concept later" and a freeze test guarding the UUIDs. KEC's
rule of 2026-10-17 settles when: a plugin's concepts are written in the
starter set, even when the code lives in the plugin
(xref:dev-komet-actions[], "Where a plugin's action concepts are written").
So those identities move into the starter set as *adopted* identities, the
tier of xref:dev-knowledge-set-identity[] for a UUID declared rather than
derived, keeping the pinned UUIDs so that nothing persisted under them
moves, and the hand-written enums give way to the generated bindings of
xref:dev-enum-concept-bindings[]. The four new concepts and
`FOOTER_OUTSIDE` are minted fresh, by derivation.

=== Surface layout terminology

indexterm:[terminology, surface layout]
indexterm:[AreaGridSettings, concept binding]
The surface layout terminology names what the family topic and this one say
in prose. A *Surface model* root holds *surface*; *surface type* (Document,
Console, Dashboard); *region* (left margin, body, right margin, body-inset,
left-half, right-half, full-bleed); *grid placement* with the fields of the
layout tier's `AreaGridSettings` that today have no concept binding,
*column index*, *row index*, *column span*, *row span*, *horizontal grow*,
*vertical grow*, *horizontal alignment*, *vertical alignment*, *margin*,
*fill width*, *fill height* and *visible* <<kl-area>>; *perspective*, a
named arrangement; *layer* (the Provenance layer, with its toggles
marginalia, commit tints, review queue, annotation marks); *view* (Reading,
Reading, Page layout); and *expansion rung* (figure, document, card, window,
screen) <<kl-expand>>. The patterns proposed: the *Surface layout pattern*
the rich interaction surface names and the rich-surface set lacks, mapping
an element by its id to a grid placement, one semantic per perspective; and
a *Surface pattern* holding a surface's type, the regions in use, its layers
and its current view.

=== Formal objects

indexterm:[terminology, formal object]
indexterm:[equation, stem block]
The lists of figures, tables and equations are queries only if the things
listed are typed. The structure set has a table kind and an image kind, no
figure, no equation, and one *title* field that serves a section's heading,
a block's title and a description term alike. Two concepts are needed. A
*Formal object* concept, with the kinds *figure*, *table* and *equation*,
marks a block as numbered and listed, and carries a *caption* distinct from
the element title, since a captioned table also has a title row and a
figure's caption is prose while its title is a label. A *Document equation*
kind under *Document block* is the AsciiDoc `stem` block, which the
structure set today keeps as an opaque block. The number of a formal object
is never stored: it is derived from the object's position in its chapter
under the folio scheme, so a reordering renumbers and a list of tables is
the query "formal objects of kind table, in document order, with caption
and folio".

=== Identity discipline

indexterm:[terminology, identity discipline]
The sets above are minted under the starter set's discipline, and not
before this topic settles.

* *Names.* Every concept and pattern is born with a fully qualified name
  under the foundation namespace, the `(IkeFoundation)` suffix, and its
  identity is `T5` over the set's UUID and that birth name, the derived tier
  of xref:dev-knowledge-set-identity[]; the six print-settings vocabularies
  are the exception, adopted under their pinned UUIDs.
* *Bindings.* The generated enums of xref:dev-enum-concept-bindings[]
  replace hand-pinned constants, and their bijection tests hold the enum and
  the concepts in step; the plugin's freeze test retires with the enums it
  guarded.
* *Fidelity.* `FoundationFidelityIT` in `ike-terms` gates the set's shape
  against its baseline; its expected counts are bumped by the exact number
  of declarations added, never rounded, and `ReferentialClosureIT` confirms
  every reference resolves.
* *Branch and checkpoint.* The declarations land on a feature sibling of
  `ike-starter-set`, finish to `main`, and publish through
  `ws:checkpoint-publish` (a `checkpoint/main-…` tag and file), which is the
  artifact the plugin and the dialect parser consume; code binds to the
  concepts only once a checkpoint carries them.
* *Settlement first.* The names in this section are proposals. KEC settles
  the topic, and the sets are minted from the settled text, so that the
  starter set never carries a vocabulary a topic later renames.

== Decisions

indexterm:[document surface, decisions]
.For KEC

|===
| Question | Proposal

| The family topic's print lineage
| Amend the Lineage section of xref:dev-interaction-surface-family[] to say
that Komet's print is the JavaFX engine over the block model, that the
Maven-built books print through Prince, and that DocBook is an export the
pipeline can emit and not a print path. The alternative is to leave the
sentence as it stands, naming a DocBook and XSL-FO chain nothing renders.

| The embedding spike's criteria
| Go when `typst-cffi` builds and loads through the foreign-function API on
every platform in the jlink image, a page recompiles within the latency an
edit tolerates, and click-to-span introspection is reachable; otherwise the
exact-preview mode, if offered, keeps the command line, a process per
compile. The alternative is to commit to embedding before the spike, which
stakes that mode on one person's crate.

| The oracle's route
| The command-line compile plus the PDF's line boxes first, since it needs
no embedding and ships with the bundled binary, with the embedded
compiler's frames replacing it when the spike succeeds. The alternative is
to wait for the frames and ship Page layout as pass one alone until then,
which is honest and leaves the view greedy for as long as the spike takes.

| An exact-preview mode
| Offer it later, as a mode beside Page layout rather than a fourth view,
rendering Typst's per-page SVG (text stays text) with PNG at display
resolution as the fallback, for the cases pass two does not cover, such as
a live figure's exact print form. The alternative is never to offer it and
let the PDF be the exact page, which is simpler now that pass two makes
Page layout agree with Typst.

| The fonts
| The Noto families, embedded by Typst and loaded by FX, so that Reading,
Page layout and the Typst page share metrics. The alternative is the theme's serif as today, with
metrics that differ between the two views.

| Where the writer library lives
| In `ike-docs`, beside the converter and the Koncept extension, consumed
by `komet-claude-plugin` as a versioned dependency like `stamp-adoc-core`.
The alternative is a writer inside the plugin, which leaves the compendium
and the printout to drift.

| One pinned Typst version
| One version in the parent's properties, the binary installed by Homebrew
on `kec` and the `ikes` agent and bundled in the jlink image, the renderer
refusing any other. The alternative is whatever `typst` is on the path,
which a 0.x minor can break without notice.

| Read as runs, edit as `RichTextArea`
| Accept the reconciliation above, which also settles the predecessor's
first decision, and amend one sentence of
xref:dev-rich-interaction-surface[]. The alternative is `RichTextArea` for
both projections with character selection mapped back to lines, at the price
of a second document model.

| Where page settings live when documents nest
| With the outermost printed document; a flowed child contributes structure
only. The alternative, letting a child carry its own section-start or folio
rules into the host, makes an assembly's pagination depend on thirty-five
files' settings.

| The four new settings concepts
| `FolioScheme`, `BindingGutter`, `SectionStart` and `AsOfLine`, plus
`FOOTER_OUTSIDE` on `PageNumberPlacement`, as starter-set concepts with
pinned ids and a new encoder version of `PrintSettings`. The alternative is
to leave the loose-leaf choices to the pipeline's CSS alone, which keeps the
screen unable to show a chapter-local folio or a gutter.

| Table terminology after CALS
| Adopt the CALS names (table group, column spec, span spec, entry, more
rows, page-wide, orientation) as the concepts, so the terminology is the
one DocBook, XSL and the pipeline already speak. The alternative is names of
our own for the same things, which reads better in a menu and costs every
reader who knows CALS a translation.

| Marginalia in print
| Printed in the outside margin, widening the page margin to wide when the
Provenance layer is on. The alternative is a Provenance layer that is
screen-only, so a reviewer's printout carries no identicons, which is
simpler and loses the review-on-paper case.
|===

== Phased delivery

indexterm:[document surface, phases]
Two tracks, independent of each other: the pipeline track puts Typst where
Prince is, and the surface track builds the document surface. The shared
writer library is the first step of both.

.Pipeline track
. *The shared writer library and the `typst` backend.* The Typst document
  model, page and furniture setup and serializer in `ike-docs`; the
  AsciidoctorJ converter registered for `typst`, `Backend.TYPST`, and
  `ike.skip.typst` in `AsciidocMojo`; standard Typst for tables, footnotes,
  cross references, index terms, bibliography and `stem`.
. *The `typst` renderer.* `RendererType.TYPST` in `RenderPdfMojo` running
  `typst compile` with `--root` and `--font-path` into `pdf-typst/`;
  `ike.skip.typst` in `PrepareRendererOutputMojo`; `assembly/typst.xml`;
  `typst` accepted by `ike.pdf.default`; the pinned Typst version on `kec`
  and the `ikes` agent; the ANF compendium rendered both ways and compared
  page by page.
. *The Koncept Typst path.* Badges as Typst drawing or embedded SVG; the
  glossary emitted by the converter; the copy to `pdf/` switched to Typst
  when the acceptance test passes, and Prince retired from CI.

.Surface track
. *The block-model writer and the bundled binary.* Komet's writer over the
  shared library; the Typst binary in the jlink image run as a process for
  print and PDF; the Page layout view beside Source and Reading, laid out by
  the paged engine over the shared page model as pass one; the fidelity
  measure against Typst's render; the document title and the current
  section in the running head; tables by
  their `cols` proportions with repeated headers and continued captions. No
  new settings.
. *The settings concepts.* `FolioScheme`, `BindingGutter`, `SectionStart`,
  `AsOfLine` and `FOOTER_OUTSIDE`; the encoder version; the freeze test; the
  generated form showing them; the writer emitting page setup, keep and
  break rules by block kind, tables keeping or flowing by size, table
  numbering by chapter and the lists of figures, tables and equations as
  outlines, and the as-of line. The Page terminology and the formal-object
  concepts minted in the starter set, the six print-settings vocabularies
  adopted under their pinned ids.
. *The area.* `StampedDocumentCard` as a host card over a stamped document
  area; figure areas inserted by named-region span; flowed and fitted child
  documents with the assembly's includes as flowed children; preferences
  nodes and view-context inheritance with the pinned badge; the expansion
  ladder through figure, document, card and window; the table as the
  expansion figure, snapping to `body` or `full-bleed` and climbing a rung
  when wider than the card; live pattern tables laid out through the table
  block and printed as typeset tables; the Surface layout terminology
  minted, with the surface-layout pattern the rich-surface set lacks; the
  predecessor's Repository panel and git operations as its phase 4.
. *Editing in place.* The edit projection as `RichTextArea` per block;
  table rows; the line match by content hash across an edit; Page layout
  re-laid out from Reading in pass one and settled in pass two from the
  oracle by the command-line route, the PDF's line boxes read by a named
  module; the writer's span alignment for the PDF's way back to a block; the predecessor's `DocumentSurface` moved to runs for reading; cells
  as blocks in the dialect parser, with nested blocks for AsciiDoc cells, so
  a cell is the subject of an edit and an annotation; the Tables terminology
  minted after CALS, and the parser emitting table group, column spec and
  entry semantics in place of the raw parameters string.
. *The embedding spike.* `typst-cffi` bound through the foreign-function
  and memory API, a native library per platform in the jlink image, measured
  against the criteria in the decisions; go, and pass two reads the
  oracle from the embedded compiler's frames with source spans, with
  incremental recompile in process and click-to-source for an exact
  preview; no go, and pass two keeps the command-line route.
. *The layout fidelity harness.* The corpus with its synthetic stress
  documents; golden Typst layouts pinned to the Typst version with the
  regeneration goal and its loud diff, after the writer's own round-trip
  tests; the headless pass-one score with per-rule attribution; the score
  as build-report Measures with a CI gate on regression; the cheap rules
  tuned first, paragraph breaking last.
. *The layout policy layer.* Keep, break, widow and orphan, placement,
  furniture and perspective overrides as concept-bound Evrete rules over
  block facts, with explicit precedence and a harness test each; consumed
  by the paged engine as constraints and emitted by the Typst writer as
  `set` and `show` rules; the algorithms, line breaking, fitting,
  hyphenation and measurement, left to the engines.
. *Print coverage.* Sidenotes in the outside margin and table notes under
  their table; the index from queried entries; equations through `mitex`;
  diagrams through `diagraph`, Smetana and the SVG exports of Komet's own
  renderers; live FX controls as exported SVG; hyphenation off inside
  tables; PDF/UA-1 and PDF/A profiles; the loose-leaf outputs re-expressed
  from the Page terminology for the Maven-built books.

The pipeline track retires Prince from the toolchain for what the writer
covers; the surface track's first phase shows a document's pages as Typst
sets them, the second gives the page its settings, the third and fourth are
the surface as the family describes it, an area among areas that is read,
printed and edited in one place, the fifth decides where Typst runs, the
sixth turns the oracle into a measuring instrument that improves pass one
over time, the seventh moves layout policy into concept-bound rules that
both engines obey, and the eighth is the print the morning's capability
list asked for, met by Typst rather than written in JavaFX.
