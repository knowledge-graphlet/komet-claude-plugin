= The Stamped Document Surface — Reading, Source, and Provenance in One Card

indexterm:[stamped document, surface]
indexterm:[STAMP provenance, reading view]
A committed AsciiDoc file is materialized into the knowledge base with a STAMP
on every line and block, one per commit that touched it, and the annotations
people have made on those lines travel with it
(xref:dev-komet-actions[], `IKE-Network/ike-issues#1307`). A reviewer reading
such a document wants two things of the surface at once: to read the document
as a document, and to see, for any sentence, where it came from and what has
been said about it. A paragraph on screen is often made of lines from several
commits, so "where it came from" has no single answer per paragraph. This
topic settles the surface that shows both: one card, a Reading view and a
Source view, and a Provenance layer over either, with selection by line so
that affirming, refuting and commenting start from the text itself.

The card is not a surface of its own kind. It is the *Document surface* of
xref:dev-interaction-surface-family[the interaction-surface family], blocks on
the twelve-track grid with prose in `body` and marginalia in a margin region,
one scroll axis, row virtualization owned by the surface, shown for a document
whose blocks were materialized from git. Journals and git documents share one
block model (xref:dev-komet-actions[]), so they share one surface type, and
what this topic adds, provenance on the text and selection by line, is what a
reviewed document needs of that surface rather than a second lineage of cards.

== What the two cards do today

indexterm:[stamped document, two cards]
The assistant plugin (`komet-claude-plugin`, package
`network.ike.komet.claude.stamp`) shows a materialized document on two
journal cards.

The *STAMP Provenance card* (`StampProvenanceCard`) is a line ledger: one row
per line with a checkbox, the line number, the introducing commit's identicon,
an annotation mark, and the text. Its toolbar holds Open, Refresh, Affirm,
Refute, Comment, Materialize and a Review queue toggle. A person ticks lines
and then chooses an act; the card computes the trailers and asks for the
signed commit.

The *Stamped Document card* (`StampedDocumentCard`) is the reading view. It
draws each block from the store as a wrapped `Label` per paragraph, with a
margin column to the left of each block holding the identicons of the STAMPs
that introduced its lines, the first date with a "+N" for the rest, and an edited or affirmed
mark. Its toolbar holds the document chooser, Refresh, Export, Commit, Discard
edit and Restore as pending. A double click on a block edits it as text, which
lands as the person's uncommitted edit under `Edit document block`.

KEC reviewed a screenshot of both on 2026-10-19 and found three faults.

* The toolbars are crowded and unreadable: every verb is a button, always
  shown, whether or not it applies. Commit, Discard edit and Restore as
  pending mean nothing until an edit is pending, and Affirm, Refute and
  Comment mean nothing until lines are ticked.
* The reading view cannot select text. A `Label` has no selection, so the
  reviewer who has just read a sentence must find it again by line number in
  the ledger to affirm or refute it.
* Provenance is hidden behind a margin tooltip. The identicons stand beside a
  whole block, and the fact that one paragraph joins lines from several commits
  shows only as "+2" after a date, with the detail in a tooltip per identicon.
  Nothing on the text itself says which words came from which commit.

The split into two cards is the root of the first two faults: the ledger has
the acts and the lines, and the reading view has the document, and neither
has both.

== One card, three views, one layer

indexterm:[stamped document, Reading view]
indexterm:[stamped document, Source view]
indexterm:[stamped document, Provenance layer]
The two cards become one *Stamped Document* card with three views of the
same `StoredDocument` and one layer that applies to any of them. KEC named
the three on 2026-10-20: Source, Reading, Page layout.

.Views and the layer
[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Part | What it shows

| Reading view
| The document rendered as a document: headings, paragraphs, lists, tables,
listings, admonitions, as the Stamped Document card draws them today, but
with each paragraph built from runs that carry their source lines (the next
section). This is the default view.

| Source view
| The document as lines: the ledger the STAMP Provenance card draws today,
one row per `StoredLine` with its number, introducing identicon, annotation
mark and text. The row is the same run the Reading view draws, so hover,
highlighting and selection behave the same in both views.

| Page layout view
| The document laid into pages by JavaFX from the stored blocks through the
paged engine, over the same page model the Typst writer uses, in two
passes: an instant greedy layout, then a re-flow of each paragraph to the
exact lines Typst set, with Typst's compile as the layout oracle, keeping
the blocks so provenance and selection survive. The pages are one
continuous scroll on the card's one axis, with a footer indicator of the
current page and no page stepper, by KEC's rule of 2026-10-20 that a Komet
surface scrolls a sequence and never steps it; nothing is edited here, and
a double click opens the block in Reading. Designed in the successor,
xref:dev-document-surface-pages[]; print itself is Typst's output.

| Provenance layer
| What the document carries beside its text: the identicon marginalia, the
commit tints, the review-queue margin marks and the annotation marks. Each is
a toggle in the layer menu; the layer is drawn over whichever view is shown.
Turning the layer off leaves a plain document. The marks are the family's
marginalia: they occupy the `right-margin` region and share a row with the
block they belong to, scrolling with it. The family draws the one scrollbar on
the left to keep the right margin clean for exactly this, so the identicon
column moves from the left of the block, where today's card draws it, to the
right margin.
|===

A document opened from a repository (the ledger's Open) and a document chosen
from the store (the reading view's chooser) are one choice: the card opens a
file, materializes it if the store does not hold it yet, and shows the store's
copy. Refresh re-reads the repository and the store together.

Switching documents is one of three gestures, by where the document is: the
chooser for one the store holds, Open file… for one on disk not yet
materialized, and Clone… for one on a remote (the repository section
below), which then offers the chooser on the result. *Close* returns the
card to its empty state, the status line asking for a document, and touches
neither the store nor the repository: the materialized document stays. A
switch or a close while an edit is pending *refuses*, the status line saying
that an edit is pending and pointing at Commit… and Discard edit, as the
action already refuses a second edit on top of an uncommitted one;
discarding is the person's decision, never a side effect of a chooser.

== The toolbar is contextual

indexterm:[stamped document, toolbar]
indexterm:[stamped document, overflow menu]
The toolbar shows what applies now and nothing else. Its fixed part is the
document chooser, the view switch and the layer menu. The view switch is
one segmented three-way selection, a radio group drawn as a single
segmented control with Source, Reading and Page layout as its segments,
exactly one selected and the arrow keys moving the selection, never three
buttons: KEC's rule of 2026-10-20 for exclusive states on a Komet toolbar
(xref:dev-document-surface-pages[], the views section). The
rest appears with the state that makes it meaningful.

.What the toolbar shows, and when
[cols="1,2", options="header"]
|===
| State | What appears

| Always
| Document chooser; the segmented Source / Reading / Page layout selection; layer menu (marginalia, tints,
review queue, annotation marks); overflow menu.

| An edit is pending
| Commit… and Discard edit, as a group with the pending-edit banner.

| A cancelled edit can be restored
| Restore as pending, in the same group, only while the restorable edit
exists.

| Lines are selected
| Nothing on the toolbar. The acts on a selection are the context menu of
the selection (below), where the reviewer's pointer already is.

| Overflow menu
| Refresh, Export…, Materialize, Open file…, Close, Show history; and the
repository verbs of the section below: Repository…, Clone…, Fetch, Pull,
Push, History. Rarely used verbs, and those that act on the whole document
or its repository rather than on what is selected.
|===

The status line stays: it is where the card says what it is doing and what
it refuses.

== Runs carry their lines

indexterm:[stamped document, TextFlow run]
indexterm:[StoredLine, run]
A paragraph is drawn as a `TextFlow` of runs, one `Text` per source line,
joined by the space AsciiDoc puts between the lines of a paragraph. Each run
holds its `StoredLine`, so the nid, the introducing and latest STAMPs, and the
annotations in force are at hand from the node under the pointer. The block
holds its `StoredBlock`, so a run knows its block and its neighbours. Inline
references (`k:` badges, cross references, links) render inside the run that
holds them, as the reading view renders them today; a line with a badge is
still one run.

`TextFlow` wraps across runs, so the document reads as prose while every word
keeps its line. This is the whole of the mechanism: the layer, the hover, the
highlighting and the selection are all functions of "which run is this". It is
also the family's rule that prose is a small per-block text control, one
block's worth, so the surface keeps row virtualization and no block ever
realizes more than itself.

Headings, list items, table cells and listing lines are runs too, each a one-
line paragraph or a line of a verbatim block. The Source view is the same
runs laid out one per row, with the number, identicon and mark columns
beside them.

== Provenance on hover

indexterm:[stamped document, hover popover]
indexterm:[stamped document, identicon highlighting]
indexterm:[stamped document, commit tints]
Hovering a run opens a popover for its line: the introducing STAMP's
identicon, author, date and state; the commit's short SHA and subject, read
from the repository when the card has one open; the latest STAMP when a later
commit changed the line; and the annotations in force, each with its kind,
author and text. The popover is the tooltip the margin shows today, moved to
the text and given the line's annotations.

Hovering an identicon in the margin highlights every run in the block that
commit introduced, so the reviewer sees at once which sentences of a paragraph
came from which commit. The "+N" after the date goes; the margin shows every
introducing identicon for the block, and hover tells them apart.

Commit tints are a toggle in the layer: each run tinted by its introducing
STAMP, the hue derived from the STAMP's identicon seed, so the same commit is
the same colour everywhere in the document. Off by default; a reviewer turns
it on to see the shape of a document's history, and off to read.

== Selection is by line

indexterm:[stamped document, line selection]
indexterm:[stamped document, context menu]
Selection works at the level annotation works at: whole lines. Dragging across
runs selects the runs the drag touched, snapped to run boundaries, in either
view; a click selects one run, a shift-click extends to a range. A selection
is a contiguous range of lines, which may cross blocks, and it is shown as a
highlight on the runs and as a mark in the margin. Snapping to lines is not a
limitation of the drawing but the meaning of the act: an annotation names a
line range in its trailer (`path:from-to@rev#hash`,
xref:dev-annotation-trailer[]), so what is selected is exactly what will be
recorded.

The right-click menu of a selection offers the acts.

.The context menu
[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Item | What it does

| Affirm…
| Prepares an affirmation of the selected lines: computes the trailers,
shows them with the lines, and asks for the signed commit. What the ledger's
Affirm button does today for ticked lines.

| Refute…
| The same with a refutation, whose text is required.

| Comment…
| The same with a comment, which takes no stance.

| Edit block
| Opens the block the selection starts in as text, the double-click edit of
today, landing as an uncommitted `Edit document block`. Disabled while an
edit is pending elsewhere in the document, as the action's refusal already
is.

| Show history
| Lists the STAMPs of the selected lines in order, with the commit subject
of each and the annotations made on them, in the same popover form as hover,
pinned until closed.
|===

Line selection sits one level below the selection the family already has to
route: selecting a block, and selecting a nested surface. The three do not
share a gesture. A drag or click on text selects lines within the block rows
it touches; a click on a block's margin or frame selects the block, as the
double-click edit does today; and a nested surface takes the gestures inside
its own frame. A line selection never promotes itself to a block selection,
and the context menu offered is the one for the level selected.

The checkboxes of the ledger are the selection of the Source view. Whether
the Source view keeps a checkbox column as a second way to build a
non-contiguous selection is one of the decisions below.

== The review queue is a margin mark

indexterm:[review queue, margin mark]
The review queue, the lines introduced but affirmed by no one, with refuted
lines kept in it, is shown as marks in the margin rather than as a tint over
the body: a mark beside each block that holds queued lines, and the queued
runs underlined when the layer's review-queue toggle is on. The reviewer scans
the margin for what is left to review and reads the text untinted. The toggle
moves from the toolbar into the layer menu, where it belongs with the other
provenance marks.

== The assistant works on the selection

indexterm:[Komet assistant, stamp_lines]
indexterm:[Komet assistant, propose_annotation]
The assistant's two provenance tools already address lines of the open
document. `stamp_lines` reads the STAMPs and annotations of a line range, and
`propose_annotation` computes the trailers for a range and hands them to the
card for the person to confirm with a signed commit. The selection becomes
their default subject: a call to `stamp_lines` with no range reads the
selected lines, so "who wrote this?" asked with a sentence selected is
answered for that sentence; a call to `propose_annotation` with no range
proposes for the selection, and the card shows the proposal on the selected
runs, with the same confirm dialog the context menu opens. The assistant
proposes and the person settles, as xref:dev-komet-actions[] rules for
everything the assistant writes.

When actions take the shape of xref:dev-komet-actions[] on this surface, the
selection is the subject an offer is made against: a block by its element
semantic, a line range by its lines. The context menu then lists the offers
the rules make for the selection, and the assistant's tools are those same
offers.

== The repository and its operations

indexterm:[stamped document, repository panel]
indexterm:[git operations, stamped document]
A document materialized from git is read and annotated on this surface, but
nothing on it says where the document comes from or lets a person move it.
KEC put it plainly on 2026-10-19: "I don't see git info commands surfaced
anywhere. Filename/Git URL? And raw git commands to load from a new git
repo, or commit and push?" Today the provenance card's status line shows the
repository-relative path, the short commit and the branch; the document card
shows the document id and its commit; the repository root is known to the
card and not shown; and the remote URL is neither shown nor stored, since
the Git source pattern records a path and a commit and nothing else. The
core's `GitRepo` reads the repository (open, head revision, branch, head
lines, lines at a revision) and every commit runs through the person's own
git command (`DocumentCommit`, `AnnotationCommit`), so identity, signing and
credentials are theirs. This section adds the panel and the operations on
that footing.

=== The Repository panel

indexterm:[Repository panel, stamped document]
A *Repository panel* opens from the toolbar's overflow menu (Repository…)
and shows, for the repository of the open document:

* the *root*, the absolute path, and the document's path within it;
* the *remotes*, each name with its URL as a clickable link, read from the
  repository's configuration;
* the *branch*, and the *HEAD* as its short sha and commit summary;
* *ahead and behind* against the branch's upstream, as git counts them
  (`git rev-list --left-right --count @{upstream}...HEAD`
  <<git-rev-list>>), or "no upstream" when the branch has none;
* the *file status* of the open document: clean, modified in the working
  tree, or carrying a pending edit in Komet, the last being the surface's
  own state rather than git's.

The panel is read from git each time it is opened and after each operation
below; it is not watched, since a stamped document changes only when a
commit lands.

=== Operations through the person's own git

indexterm:[git operations, refusal over retry]
Every operation runs the person's `git` as a process, as the commits do
today, never JGit for anything that authenticates or writes a ref, and each
one reports git's own output in the status line and refuses rather than
retries. The operations are the plain git verbs <<git-clone>> <<git-fetch>>
<<git-pull>> <<git-push>>, named as git names them.

.The operations
[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Operation | What it does, and what it refuses

| Clone…
| Asks for a URL and a folder, runs `git clone` into it, and offers to open
the document chooser on the result. It refuses a folder that is not empty,
as git does <<git-clone>>, and it never clones *into* an existing tree: a
synced working set in `ike-dev` arrives as a bare tree and is materialized
at the open gesture, and a clone into it is the way to corrupt it
(`IKE-Network/ike-issues#1057`; the rule is recorded in the workspace's own
instructions <<ike-dev-claude>>). When the chosen folder is inside a synced
workspace, the panel says so and names the manifest the new repository must
be added to.

| Fetch
| `git fetch` on the current remote; updates ahead and behind.

| Pull
| `git pull --ff-only` <<git-pull>>: fast-forward only, and when the branch
has diverged git's refusal is shown as git wrote it, with no merge and no
rebase offered. Divergence is a decision for the person at their terminal,
not for a card.

| Push
| Shows the ahead count and the remote and branch it will push to, asks for
confirmation, then `git push` <<git-push>>. A rejected push is reported as
git reports it; nothing is forced.

| Commit…
| As already designed: the person's signed commit of a document edit or an
annotation, through their git.
|===

=== The History view

indexterm:[History view, stamped document]
A *History view*, from the overflow menu, lists commits for the open file
and, on a switch, for the whole repository, each with its short sha,
author, date and subject, from `git log` <<git-log>>. Selecting a commit
highlights the runs it introduced in the Reading and Source views, the
same highlighting the margin identicon gives on hover, so a person can read
a commit as a set of lines rather than as a diff; where the card already has
the commit's STAMP, the two are one object.

=== Authentication, and the Dock-launch trap

indexterm:[Dock launch, environment]
indexterm:[git credentials, Komet]
Komet launched from the Dock does not inherit the shell's environment, the
trap the workspace already records for IntelliJ and `JAVA_HOME`
<<ike-dev-claude>>. So nothing here depends on `PATH` or on environment
variables: authentication comes from git's own configuration as git
resolves it, the credential helper for HTTPS <<git-credentials>>, the SSH
configuration and the agent socket it names for SSH, and the signing key
git's configuration names; the card passes a repository and a verb and
nothing else. A failure, a missing helper, an unreachable agent, a rejected
key, is surfaced with git's message and not interpreted.

=== Terminology

The Git source pattern gains a referent: a *Git repository* concept, with
its *remote URL* and *default branch*, which a document's Git source refers
to, so that a document in the store knows the repository it came from and
not only a path and a commit within it. The panel's remotes, ahead and
behind, and file status are read live and not stored. The names here are
git's, except *Repository panel*, *History view* and *pending edit in
Komet*, which are this surface's own.

== Beyond the scroll

indexterm:[stamped document, successor]
This topic settles the document as one scroll. What a document needs beyond
that, a Page layout view with running heads and folios, margins and a binding
gutter, the card as a host of a document area and the insertion of figure and
document areas into it, Komet's own print engine raised to Prince-equivalent
quality, and editing in place within limits, is the successor topic
xref:dev-document-surface-pages[]. It also proposes the answer to the first
decision below, runs for reading and the `RichTextArea` for editing, and it
carries the references for the terms this topic borrows: _marginalia_ and
the _running head_ and _folio_ are the book's, as the style manuals and the
typographers describe them (`chicago2017`, `harts2014`, `bringhurst2012`
in the successor's References), and the _ledger_ is the Source view's own
word for a line-per-row listing. _Reading view_, _Source view_ and
_Provenance layer_ are names this topic coins; nothing in the family or in
the lineage of printed books names a toggled layer of provenance marks.

== Decisions

indexterm:[stamped document, decisions]
.For KEC
[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Question | Proposal

| Own run-level selection on `TextFlow`, or the `RichTextArea`
| Build selection on `TextFlow` ourselves: a drag over runs, snapped to run
boundaries, with a highlight drawn behind the selected runs. The selection
is by line, so character-level selection is not needed, and the incubator
`RichTextArea` would bring its own model, its own editing and its own
styling for a document that is not edited in place. The family already keeps
it out of the document for a second reason: it couples virtualization to
owning its scrollbar, and the surface owns the one scroll axis
(xref:dev-interaction-surface-family[]). The cost is a few hundred lines of
mouse handling. The alternative is to adopt the `RichTextArea` per block, for
the Reading view only, and map its character selection back to lines; it
gives copy and native selection for free, at the price of a second document
model to keep in step with the store.

| Granularity of annotation
| Lines, as the trailer grammar already fixes. A selection snaps to whole
lines, and an annotation on part of a line annotates the line. Sub-line
ranges would need a new trailer form and a content hash per fragment; nothing
asks for them yet.

| Clone into the synced workspace
| Allow Clone into a new folder under `ike-dev` only with the panel naming
the origins manifest the repository must be added to, and never into an
existing tree. The alternative is to refuse any folder inside a synced
workspace from Komet and leave such clones to the terminal, which is safer
and one step more for the common case.

| Whether the Source view keeps the checkbox column
| Drop it. Click and shift-click select a range; a command-click adds a run
to the selection, which gives the non-contiguous ticking the checkboxes gave,
without a column. Keep it if KEC wants a selection that survives scrolling
away and back as visibly as a ticked box does.
|===

== Phased delivery

indexterm:[stamped document, phases]
. *Toolbar and card merge.* One card; the ledger becomes its Source view;
  the contextual toolbar with the overflow and layer menus; Open and the
  chooser become one choice; Close, and the refusal to switch or close
  while an edit is pending. No change to what is drawn in either view.
. *Runs and hover.* Paragraphs as `TextFlow` runs carrying `StoredLine`; the
  hover popover; margin-identicon highlighting; commit tints as a layer; the
  review queue as margin marks; the marginalia moved to the right margin.
. *Selection and the context menu.* Run-level selection in both views; the
  context menu with Affirm, Refute, Comment, Edit block and Show history;
  the checkbox column retired or kept per the decision above.
. *The repository.* The Repository panel with root, remotes, branch, HEAD,
  ahead and behind and file status; Clone, Fetch, Pull fast-forward only and
  Push through the person's own git, each reporting git's output and
  refusing rather than retrying; the History view with commit-to-lines
  highlighting; the Git repository concept referenced from the Git source
  pattern.
. *The assistant.* `stamp_lines` and `propose_annotation` default to the
  selection; proposals shown on the selected runs; the selection as the
  subject of action offers once xref:dev-komet-actions[] lands on this
  surface.

Each phase ships on its own; the first removes the faults KEC saw without
touching how text is drawn, the second and third are where the surface
earns its name, and the fourth gives the document its repository.
